A note from the founder

SPAI began because I needed an assistant that did not exist.

I live with ADHD, and one of the hardest parts is not a lack of ideas. It is having more ideas, thoughts, responsibilities and parts of my own life than I can reliably organise, preserve and return to.

My attention is often pulled toward whatever feels most immediate, interesting or rewarding. Important tasks, decisions and ideas can quietly disappear into the background. I can understand the full shape of something I want to build, but struggle to keep every decision, checkpoint, unfinished task and next step active in my mind over weeks or months.

Repetitive administration, documentation, checking and small routine steps can also become a wall between an idea and a finished result. I knew that much of this could be helped: ideas could be captured before they disappeared, decisions could be preserved, projects could retain checkpoints, and context could be carried forward instead of reconstructed every time.

I created SPAI to try to become the mediator between my dopamine and real life.

I wanted something that could help translate intention into action, create structure when my mind struggled to maintain it, remember what mattered, and help me return to what I had started when motivation alone was not enough.

But an assistant capable of helping with so much of a person’s life would become deeply personal. I did not want to hand my memory, files, projects, private thoughts, habits and future plans to a company in exchange for convenience. I wanted to choose what it knew, what it remembered, what it could access, and when it needed to ask first.

I could not find the assistant I believed people should be able to own. So I started building SPAI.

The principle behind SPAI

WE THE PEOPLE PUT HUMANS FIRST

AI should expand human possibility — not reduce people to data, labour costs or recurring income.

Artificial intelligence is moving into our homes, workplaces, files, memories, conversations and everyday lives. It could become one of the greatest tools humanity has ever created. It could also become one of the most intrusive technologies ever placed into ordinary people’s lives.

Too often, convenience comes with a hidden exchange: private information sits inside somebody else’s system, habits become data, attention becomes a product, and personal life becomes dependent on a platform the user does not own or control.

The system should learn how to help the person.

Millions of people have ideas, ambitions and abilities that existing systems fail to support. Some struggle with attention, memory, organisation, confidence, disability, language, technical knowledge, time or access to expensive tools.

Technology often tells people they must first understand the system before the system can help them. SPAI is being built around the opposite belief.

SPAI is for people who want the power to know how, without knowing how.

Technology should adapt to people.

The future should not reward only those who already work in the way existing systems expect. Technology should help preserve thoughts that may otherwise be lost, create checkpoints that may otherwise be skipped, and connect today’s effort to yesterday’s decisions and tomorrow’s goal.

The assistant should adapt its support without taking control away from the person receiving it.

Accessible must mean more than affordable.

A product is not accessible when it is confusing, requires knowledge most people were never given the chance to learn, works only on expensive hardware, treats disability as an afterthought, traps usefulness behind permanent subscriptions, or makes privacy the price of participation.

Powerful technology should not belong only to people who already have power.

Open source built the future.

Open source made extraordinary progress possible because people shared knowledge, published tools and allowed others to build upon their work. But openness should not mean talented people must donate years of work while somebody else captures all of the value.

SPAI wants to help prove that open creation and fair monetisation can exist together.

Open source should be a ladder for creators — not a demand for free labour.

Built for people, not workforce replacement.

SPAI is being built for individuals, families, creators, workers, students, communities and small businesses. It is not being built as workforce-replacement infrastructure for large corporations.

An individual should be free to use their own SPAI as an extension of themselves. The power should remain with the person. AI should increase the reach and ability of a human being, not become an excuse to declare that the human being no longer has value.

People are not a revenue source.

SPAI must make money to survive, support development, maintain infrastructure and pay creators. But money should sustain the mission. It should never replace it.

The goal is to build a successful company without becoming the kind of company SPAI was created to challenge — and to remain accountable to the people it serves.

SPAI IS HERE TO GIVE PEOPLE POWER

The power to preserve an idea. The power to continue where you left off. The power to create structure when structure is difficult. The power to turn intention into action. The power to use technology without surrendering ownership of your life.

There is nothing ordinary about what a person may be capable of when they are finally given the right tools.
